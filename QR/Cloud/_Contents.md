# Cloud

## What is Cloud?

**Simple answer**
Cloud is renting computing resources — servers, storage, databases, networking — over the internet from a provider (like AWS, Azure, or GCP), instead of buying and running your own physical hardware. You pay for what you use, and the provider handles the underlying infrastructure.

**Deeper answer**
Cloud computing is a model for delivering on-demand IT resources as a metered, self-service utility over a network. Key characteristics (per NIST's definition) that distinguish it from traditional hosting:

- **On-demand self-service** — provision resources without human interaction with the provider.
- **Broad network access** — available over the network via standard mechanisms (APIs, consoles).
- **Resource pooling** — provider's resources are pooled to serve multiple tenants, with resources dynamically assigned.
- **Rapid elasticity** — capacity can scale out/in quickly, often automatically, to match demand.
- **Measured service** — usage is metered and billed, giving pay-as-you-go economics.

Beyond the definition, a strong answer usually connects it to *why it matters*: it shifts capital expenditure (buying hardware) to operational expenditure (paying for usage), removes undifferentiated heavy lifting (racking servers, patching hypervisors), and enables service models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and deployment models (public, private, hybrid, multi-cloud) that let teams choose the right level of abstraction and control for their workload.
